## 13 — stability and reproducibility
Question: is the demonstrated level and running style repeatable under today's relevant conditions, independent of finishing-place stability?
Inputs: 02–06, 08–11, field size, full-field context, condition fingerprint, layoff/weight, incident facts, normalized class/opponent evidence (33–42), today's conditions (43–57). Source path: common archive-materials / NAR pinned CSV candidate / validated prior result; benchmark route 05; no extra request per item.
Computation:
1. Whole near-5 and near-10 starts, record valid and missing counts, wins/top3 descriptive only; preserve DNF and documented incidents as distinct status, not fabricated slow time. Historical relevant subset selected by conditions independently of outcome.
2. For each matched cohort, condition-adjusted clock residual = horse elapsed seconds minus validated cohort benchmark seconds. Calculate median and median absolute deviation (MAD) across comparable runs, plus median winner gap and upper-tail gap; record low performance level separately from low variability. Winner gap alone depends on opponent/pace, not transferable raw ability. Final3f relative to same-race distribution where complete. Field-size-normalized positions and early/final turn intervals support style reproducibility; no speed inference from rank.
3. Separate overall, relevant-condition and incident-affected histories. Do not silently discard poor runs; show both all and contextual subsets, reasons. A consistently weak horse is stable, not strong. A high-variance horse with documented condition effects may be conditionally reproducible.
4. Research v0 require >=3 comparable valid prior runs before reporting dispersion as more than sparse; >=5 preferred. With fewer, return limited evidence or unknown. No fixed MAD threshold or weight; validate per circuit and condition. Evidence outputs: sample keys/count, median residual/MAD, gap distribution, early/late position variability, contextual exceptions, reliability and current applicability; no grade.
Tests: same finish with unlike conditions not pooled; repeated poor runs not strong; one spectacular run not stable; missing and DNF not zero; repeated runs no duplication; interval corner positions not falsely exact.

## 14 — growth and decline
Question: does comparable recent performance improve or deteriorate, rather than just finishing rank?
Inputs: same validated residual/gaps/final3f, relative pace, class/opponents, layoff, weight/body weight, age, condition, reported incidents. Source: 01–13 common packet + later class/condition and personnel/fitness routes. No age or trainer reputation alone.
Computation: order all eligible runs chronologically; compare most recent up to 3 vs earlier up to 3 nonoverlapping comparable runs, each with >=2 valid runs as research floor. Show actual elapsed days and count. Directional indicators: lower adjusted elapsed residual, lower comparable winner gap, better same-race relative final3f, ability to maintain level against stronger verified class/opposition. Compute recent median minus earlier median separately; lower residual/gap is improvement, relative final3f direction defined explicitly. Avoid combining unlike units. Trend disagreement => MIXED; sparse => INSUFFICIENT; long gap, new surface, class change, equipment or incident are explanatory context not automatic penalties or erasure. Do not assert change when measurement resolution/cohort uncertainty exceeds observed difference; calibrate cutoffs via walk-forward. Output direction evidence per feature, source keys, context and uncertainty, not automatic score.
Tests: class rise with worse place but better residual; drop in opposition with win not necessarily growth; one run not trend; returning from layoff not automatically decline; later race forbidden; no old LAB score.

## 15 — latent ability
Question: is there specific underexpressed performance likely to become relevant under today's conditions?
Inputs: 03–11, 13–14, official incident facts, stronger-opponent evidence (33–42), course compatibility (43–57), pace scenarios (58–77), condition changes (93–100). Prior public mine-ta research concepts of losing strongly, race flow, improvement in conditions are hypotheses, not proprietary formula.
Evidence chains:
A) narrow gap or strong condition-adjusted performance despite poor finish vs verified strong rivals;
B) adverse race flow with resilient position/finish or late recovery, verified race lap plus corner intervals and final3f; race-level lap never individual split;
C) official reported trouble followed by demonstrable recovery; no inferred trouble from slow start or poor placing;
D) repeated improvement in comparable performance before results catch up;
E) historical strong run in conditions that return today, with evidence of current ability rather than old peak alone.
Each chain requires observed facts, alternative explanations, current relevance, and explicit confidence. One strong chain can form a candidate; no mandatory two-factor rule, no credit for unobserved facts, no popularity-based identification. Distinguish real demonstrated ability from proposed counterfactual upside; do not assign invented seconds saved or probability. Output HYPOTHESIS_SUPPORTED / MIXED / INSUFFICIENT and exact evidence IDs; later EYE determination separately uses its agreed odds gate after ability assessment. No duplicate credit for same late-run or gap fact.
Tests: fastest final3f after slow pace not automatic hidden ability; no official incident report is UNKNOWN; condition return not proven if current form weak; strong opponent subsequently wins only if that result public by cutoff; single evidence chain not mechanically transformed into grade.

## 16 — official ratings
Question: what has an official handicapper assessed the horse to have demonstrated, and under which conditions?
Distinct from LAB grades and private estimates. Sources confirmed via official public indexed pages:
- JRA overview https://www.jra.go.jp/datafile/ranking/index.html
- JRA G1 pre-ratings https://www.jra.go.jp/datafile/ranking/g1/2026.html and dated announcement for Japan Cup https://www.jra.go.jp/keiba/g1/jc.html (Nov 23, 2026), table https://www.jra.go.jp/datafile/ranking/g1/2026/jc.html
- JRA race-specific ratings https://www.jra.go.jp/datafile/ranking/jyusyo/2026.html and dated 2026-11-26 release https://www.jra.go.jp/news/202611/112604.html for Nov 21–23 races
- JRA monthly revisions https://www.jra.go.jp/datafile/ranking/jyusyo/2026/11.html notes revisions after international ranking publication; do NOT backfill latest revised rating into earlier cutoff.
- NAR dirt graded race ratings https://www.keiba.go.jp/dirtgraderace/2026/rating/dirtgraderacerating/index.html and pre-ratings https://www.keiba.go.jp/dirtgraderace/2026/rating/prerating/index.html (e.g. https://www.keiba.go.jp/dirtgraderace/2026/rating/prerating/1103_JBCclassic.html)
- IFHA dated editions https://www.ifhaonline.org/Default.asp?area=0&section=Resources&story=1239 (Nov 12, 2026; performances through Nov 8) and https://www.ifhaonline.org/default.asp?area=1&section=racing
JRA official definition uses performance with weight and margins. Ratings primarily cover specified open/graded race finishers; G1/Jpn1 through 6th and other open/graded through 4th generally eligible, others may be reference; NAR JpnI pre-rating scope also includes eligible local graded performances at 100+ under its stated criteria. No blanket coverage for ordinary LOCAL or maiden runners. Distinguish original race performance, annual highest value, pre-rating, world ranking, registration selection rating and reference figures. Do not average different scopes or treat them as independent votes. Source identity match requires official ID or verified name+birth/country/horse profile and exact performance race; English transliteration alone insufficient.
Packet: horse identity, authority, rating_type, value (official pound scale), race/date or defined period, surface/distance band and source, published_at (date granularity retained), source_checked_at, revision/version, reference status, identity/eligibility, applicability to target. Source row and dated public evidence needed; same-day date-only publication cannot prove before cutoff. Use only versions already available at cutoff. Missing = UNKNOWN/NOT_PUBLISHED as applicable, not zero. Treat peak as past achievement, not today's forecast. Compare recent race-specific ratings and best comparable distance/surface ratings, not only maximum. Weight/sex allowances and class comparisons must use authority's definitions, not improvised point conversions. Do not use ranking order as LAB order.
Indexed public example: Danon Decile appears 124 I for autumn Tenno Sho in JRA 2026 Japan Cup pre-rating; IFHA Nov 12 edition also shows 124. These are not two independent performance wins. Latest monthly revision note proves why versioning matters. Direct official pages returned cache misses in this environment; indexed excerpts only, not full-table ingestion or identity proof. No rating values for unlisted horses are estimated.
Tests: published after target rejected; revised later version rejected; two authorities same performance no double credit; past peak on different distance not automatic; no rating not weak; duplicate or unbound identity blocked; reference value tagged, not standard. No live rating acquisition until usage/access verified.
